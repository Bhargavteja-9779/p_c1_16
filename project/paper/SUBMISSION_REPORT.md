## 4. Why it could be accepted, and the honest risks

**Reasons it fits *Critical Care* and could be accepted**
1. *Scope and readership:* sepsis early warning is core critical-care territory, and the journal regularly publishes external validations of ICU ML models (15 recent ones are in `literature/journal_papers_matrix.csv`). This paper answers the question those papers leave open.
2. *A practical message intensivists can act on:* a model from another hospital lost about twice as much sensitivity as one from another unit of the same hospital, and neither harmonisation nor label-free monitoring fixed or detected it. Local labelled verification is needed.
3. *Methodological rigour unusual for the field:* staged pre-specification with frozen code, five confirmation seeds, multiplicity control, a leakage audit, a number-integrity audit, and a reproduction of the published baseline.
4. *Transparency:* every failed test is reported, with no "state-of-the-art" or overclaiming. Open data and code allow full reproduction.
5. *Fast journal:* verified median submission-to-acceptance of 66 days (318 research articles; JIF 11.4).

**Risks that could lead to rejection (do not ignore)**
1. **Only two hospital systems.** This is the largest risk. Reviewers may consider the evidence too narrow for a high-impact clinical journal.
2. **Modest and model-dependent effect.** For gradient boosting, D is 0.026 AUROC overall and is not significant on pre-onset hours alone. It is not confirmed in the static task or the exploratory hypotension task.
3. **The novelty is a design and a finding, not a new method.** The independent blind novelty score was 13/25. Simulated reviewers in round 1 suggested an informatics journal (npj Digital Medicine, JAMIA, JMIR Medical Informatics) as an alternative.
4. **Simulated review, two rounds:** round 1 gave 5/5/5 (all major revision). After revision, round 2 gave 6 (methods), 4 (novelty) and 6 (clarity), all major revision. The novelty reviewer leaned towards "reject with transfer" for *Critical Care* specifically. Gate G10 (two clean rounds) is **not** passed. The methods reviewer checked 20 numbers against the result files and found 0 errors.
5. **Unverified item:** the JCR quartile of Critical Care was not verifiable (paywalled), although the JIF of 11.4 is on the official page.

**Bottom line:** I cannot honestly say it *will* be accepted by *Critical Care*. The independent reviewers rate it major revision, and the novelty reviewer judged it below the journal's bar for clinical significance.

What it has going for it: it is rigorous, honest, reproducible, clinically relevant and verified number-by-number. What works against it: two hospitals only, a modest and model-dependent effect, and a design contribution rather than a clinical one.

Recommended strategy:
1. Submit to *Critical Care* as planned, if the authors want to try the fast, high-IF option. The cover letter is honest about scope.
2. Prepare the same package for *npj Digital Medicine*, *JAMIA* or *Critical Care Explorations*, which the reviewers saw as a better fit. Those journals were not part of the original verified ≤ 72-day turnaround screen, so their turnaround is UNVERIFIED.
3. Before any submission, the authors should complete the declarations and repository DOI. Ideally they should also add the remaining analyses listed in `review/round_2.md`: source-transferred thresholds, ATC/CBPE estimators, repeated splits and a flow diagram.
